## LAB 2 — IOC Analysis

Formula:
IOC = ΣR / N

| Item | Sum | IOC | Suggested interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Q1 | 4 | 0.80 | generally acceptable alignment; retain with contextual review |
| Q2 | 1 | 0.20 | weak alignment; review/revise |
| Q3 | 5 | 1.00 | strong alignment; retain |
| Q4 | 3 | 0.60 | moderate alignment; retain/review depending on item clarity and expert comments |
| Q5 | -1 | -0.20 | poor alignment; revise/remove |

Important teaching point:
Do not teach a single numerical cutoff as an absolute rule. IOC is one item of content-validity evidence and should be considered together with item wording, construct coverage, and expert comments.

Suggested debrief questions:
- Why is the denominator the number of experts?
- Can two items with the same IOC require different actions?
- What additional evidence would improve the revision decision?

## LAB 3 — Reliability Analysis

Method selection:
- Multi-item Likert scale → Cronbach's alpha
- Dichotomous 0/1 knowledge test → KR-20
- Multiple assessors scoring performance → inter-rater reliability
- Same instrument at two time points → test–retest reliability

Synthetic Likert dataset:
Expected Cronbach's alpha ≈ 0.902

Core interpretation:
The items show high internal consistency in this synthetic pilot dataset.

What this does NOT establish:
- it does not prove content validity;
- it does not prove construct validity;
- it does not prove the instrument measures the intended construct correctly;
- it does not establish stability over time unless test–retest evidence is available.

Teaching message:
High reliability ≠ high validity.

Suggested debrief:
Ask students to distinguish:
"consistent measurement" from "correct/valid measurement".

## LAB 4 — Statistical Data Analysis

Synthetic pre/post dataset expected descriptive statistics:

Pre:
Mean = 6.00
Sample SD ≈ 1.13

Post:
Mean ≈ 7.92
Sample SD ≈ 0.79

Mean change:
≈ +1.92

SD of individual change:
≈ 0.51

Optional advanced extension:
paired t-test t(11) ≈ 12.89, p < .001

The inferential extension should be labeled ADVANCED / OPTIONAL unless it is explicitly included in the approved lesson scope.

Core interpretation:
The synthetic learners' post scores are descriptively higher than pre scores.

Do NOT conclude:
"The program caused the improvement"
unless the evaluation design supports causal attribution.

Suggested debrief:
- What does the mean tell us?
- Why do we also need SD?
- What can a pre/post comparison support?
- What cannot it establish by itself?
- How would evaluation design affect the strength of the conclusion?

## Assessment emphasis

Prioritize:
1. method selection
2. calculation accuracy
3. interpretation quality
4. evidence-boundary awareness
5. defensibility of the decision

A correct number with unsupported interpretation should not receive full credit.
