## A. Evidence expansion

### Measurement / validity / reliability
ER-09 Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (AERA, APA, NCME, 2014). Use as authoritative measurement-standard reference. Core writing rule: validity concerns evidence supporting interpretations/uses of scores; reliability/precision is necessary evidence but is not interchangeable with validity.

ER-10 Lynn, M. R. (1986). Determination and quantification of content validity. Nursing Research, 35(6), 382–385. PMID 3640358. DOI 10.1097/00006199-198611000-00017. Use for historical/methodological content-validity discussion. Do not convert this into an unsupported universal IOC cutoff.

ER-11 Polit, D. F., Beck, C. T., & Owen, S. V. (2007). Is the CVI an acceptable indicator of content validity? Appraisal and recommendations. Research in Nursing & Health, 30(4), 459–467. DOI 10.1002/nur.20199. Use to distinguish item/scale content-validity evidence and expert relevance judgments. Do not conflate CVI with IOC.

### Triangulation / mixed evidence
ER-12 BetterEvaluation. Triangulation. Use for undergraduate explanation of data, investigator, theory and methodological triangulation and for the principle that triangulation can confirm, refute, enrich or explain rather than mechanically “prove truth.”
ER-13 BetterEvaluation. Choose methods and processes. Use for evaluation-matrix logic: key evaluation questions should drive methods, sources, feasibility and planned integration.
ER-14 Bamberger, M. (2012). Introduction to mixed methods in impact evaluation. InterAction. Use for complementary QUANT/QUAL evidence and systematic integration/triangulation.

### Evidence rule before prose freeze
- Search and bind an implementation/process evaluation source before Chapter 6 prose freeze.
- Add current measurement source(s) for reliability/measurement error and content validity where appropriate.
- IOC is taught as a content-alignment procedure familiar in Thai educational practice; textbook must explicitly distinguish IOC from CVI and from the broader validity argument.
- Never state “IOC ≥ X means the instrument is valid” as a universal rule without a source, context and limitation.
